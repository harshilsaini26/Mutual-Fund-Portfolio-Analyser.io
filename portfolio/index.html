    Your portfolio

    What your funds are worth, and what they own

    Pick the funds you hold and enter what you put in, as lump sums or SIPs, and anything you sold. The figures are worked out in this browser from the prices and holdings this site publishes; nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

    Your funds and what you put in

    Direct and Regular plans are listed: pick the one on your statement. Enter a sale as the amount you received; a switch is a sale here and a purchase in the other fund. Exit loads and tax on a sale are not taken off.

    XIRRA yearly return for money that went in on different dates and in different amounts, such as a SIP. Each amount is counted from its own date. For one purchase held more than a year it equals the annualised return; over less than a year it can look extreme, because a short gain is scaled up to a year.More in the glossary · Zerodha VarsityPortfolio overlapHow much of two funds' portfolios is the same companies. For each company both hold, the smaller of its two weights is counted, and the counts are added: 100% means identical portfolios, 0% nothing in common. Funds with a high overlap tend to rise and fall together.More in the glossary · Zerodha Varsity

    Your funds

    What you own, through all your funds

    Look-throughSeeing past funds to the companies inside them. Each fund's holdings are weighted by the money in that fund and added up, so a company held by several funds shows its total. It describes what is owned in the end, which a list of funds alone does not.More in the glossary · Zerodha Varsity, SEBI

    From each fund's latest disclosure, weighted by what your holding in it is worth.

    In hindsight: the same money in other funds

    Each fund beside others of its category, picked after the fact by the rule named beside each: what the same amounts on the same dates would be worth today. It looks back, and says nothing of what comes next.

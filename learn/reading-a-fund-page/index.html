    Reading a fund page here

    What each part of a fund page on this site shows, in the order it appears, and where each figure comes from.

    This explains how things work. It is not advice about what to buy, sell or hold.

    The header

    The top of the page names the fund, its fund house, category and plan, with the latest NAV (net asset value) and its change on the day. Below come the facts: fund house, category, plan, launch date, fund size and the span of prices on record. Then tiles: the Annualised return over one, three and five years, the Rank in category, the deepest fall, volatility, size and Expense ratio (TER).

    Performance, growth and price

    Performance sets the fund's return, volatility, deepest fall, Sharpe ratio and other measures beside its Benchmark and Total Return Index over one, three and five years. Growth shows what ₹10,000 became over the fund's whole record, and Price shows the NAV itself, with buttons to narrow the period.

    Returns and peers

    Returns sets the fund's return in each period against its benchmark's. Peers places the fund among the funds of its category on return, risk, falls and cost, with where it stands in each and its quartile.

    Falls and consistency

    Falls shows how far below its last high the fund stood on each day, its Deepest fall (maximum drawdown), and how long it took to recover. Consistency shows the Rolling returns of every three-year stretch, not only the one ending today.

    Holdings and every figure

    Holdings shows the Asset mix, the largest holdings and the Sectors, from the fund's latest Portfolio disclosure, or from an aggregator's page where marked. Every figure lists all the computed measures for every period in one table.

    Dates, sources and gaps

    Each section says how old its data is and where it came from. A figure that could not be computed shows a dash with the reason, never a zero, because a zero would be a claim. Every figure describes the past.

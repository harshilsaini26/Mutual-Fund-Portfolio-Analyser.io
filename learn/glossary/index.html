    Glossary

    Every term this site uses, in a few plain sentences, with the pages each was checked against.

    Annualised return

    The steady yearly rate, compounding, that would take an investment from its starting value to its ending value. ₹10,000 that became ₹15,000 in three years returned about 14.5% a year. Annualising lets periods of different lengths be set side by side.

    Related: XIRR, Rolling returns

    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/measuring-mutual-fund-returns/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    Asset mix

    How a fund's money is split across kinds of assets: shares, bonds and other debt, cash and its equivalents, and sometimes gold or units of other funds. Shares respond mostly to company earnings, debt to interest rates and credit, so the mix shapes how a fund behaves.

    Related: Sectors

    Benchmark and Total Return Index

    The index a fund names as its yardstick, chosen to match what it invests in. Since 2018 SEBI has required funds to measure themselves against the Total Return Index (TRI) version, which counts the dividends the index's companies pay as well as their price changes, as a fund's own return does.

    Related: Index fund

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jan-2018/1515066187290.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/mutual-benchmarking/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    Deepest fall (maximum drawdown)

    The largest drop in a fund's NAV from a high to a later low. A NAV that rose to ₹200 and fell to ₹160 had a 20% drawdown: the worst loss a buyer at the peak would have seen. This site also shows how long the fund took to climb back, or that it has not yet.

    Related: Volatility

    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/know-your-fund/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    Direct plan

    A plan bought from the fund house with no distributor; since 1 January 2013 SEBI has required one beside every Regular plan. It holds the same portfolio, under the same manager, but pays no distribution commission, so its expense ratio is lower and its NAV, kept separately, grows a little faster.

    Related: Regular plan, Expense ratio (TER)

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    • AMFI www.amfiindia.com/investor/knowledge-center-info?zoneName=DirectPlan checked 02 Oct 2026
    Expense ratio (TER)

    The total expense ratio: a fund's yearly running cost, as a share of its assets. It covers management, administration, distribution and similar costs. It is not billed separately; a small part is taken from the fund's assets each day, so the published NAV is already net of it. SEBI sets the most a fund may charge.

    Related: Direct plan, Regular plan

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    • AMFI www.amfiindia.com/investor/knowledge-center-info?zoneName=expenseRatio checked 02 Oct 2026
    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/mutual-fund-expense-ratio-direct-and-regular-plans/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    Fund house (AMC)

    The asset management company that invests a fund's money. In India a mutual fund is set up as a trust: a sponsor establishes it, trustees oversee it for the unitholders, the AMC manages the investments and a custodian holds the securities. The fund, its AMC and its custodian are each registered with or approved by SEBI.

    Related: Units

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    Fund size (AUM)

    Assets under management: the total value of the money a fund manages. AMFI publishes each scheme's average for every quarter, and that average is the fund size this site shows. Size says how much money a fund runs, not how it has done.

    Related: Fund house (AMC)

    • AMFI www.amfiindia.com/aum-data/average-aum checked 02 Oct 2026
    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/how-to-analyse-an-equity-mutual-fund/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    Growth and IDCW options

    Two ways a plan can handle what it earns. Under Growth the fund keeps it, and the gain shows as a rising NAV. Under IDCW, which SEBI renamed from "dividend" in April 2021, the fund pays part out, and the NAV falls by the amount paid. A payout can include some of the investor's own capital.

    Related: NAV (net asset value)

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    • AMFI www.mutualfundssahihai.com/en/idcw-plans-simplifying-income-and-capital-distributions-mutual-funds checked 02 Oct 2026
    Index fund

    A fund that holds the shares of an index, such as the Nifty 50, in the same proportions, aiming to match the index rather than beat it. No one picks stocks, so its costs are usually lower than an active fund's. Tracking error measures how closely it follows the index. This site uses one as a stand-in benchmark.

    Related: Benchmark and Total Return Index

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/introduction-to-index-funds/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    Large, mid and small cap

    Size bands for listed companies, set by SEBI: large cap is the 1st to 100th company by full market value, mid cap the 101st to 250th, and small cap the 251st onwards. AMFI publishes the list every six months, from data at the end of June and December.

    Related: SEBI fund categories

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2017/1509103633344.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/the-equity-scheme-part-1/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    Look-through

    Seeing past funds to the companies inside them. Each fund's holdings are weighted by the money in that fund and added up, so a company held by several funds shows its total. It describes what is owned in the end, which a list of funds alone does not.

    Related: Portfolio overlap, Portfolio disclosure

    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/the-mutual-fund-portfolio/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    Lump sum

    Money put into a fund in one go, as a single purchase, rather than in instalments. The units are priced at the NAV of the day the purchase counts for. A lump sum and a SIP can both be used in either plan, Direct or Regular.

    Related: SIP (systematic investment plan), Units

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    NAV (net asset value)

    The price of one unit of a fund: the value of everything the fund holds, less what it owes, divided by the number of units in issue. It changes every business day as the prices of the holdings move, and every fund publishes it daily, on its own website and AMFI's.

    Related: Units, Direct plan

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    • AMFI www.amfiindia.com/investor/knowledge-center-info?zoneName=NetAssetValueNAV checked 02 Oct 2026
    Portfolio disclosure

    The list of everything a fund holds, with each holding's quantity, value and share of the fund. Funds publish it every month, within ten days of month end, on their own website and AMFI's; debt schemes also publish every fortnight. This site reads these lists, or an aggregator's copy where marked.

    Related: Look-through

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    Portfolio overlap

    How much of two funds' portfolios is the same companies. For each company both hold, the smaller of its two weights is counted, and the counts are added: 100% means identical portfolios, 0% nothing in common. Funds with a high overlap tend to rise and fall together.

    Related: Look-through

    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/the-mutual-fund-portfolio/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    Rank in category

    Where a fund stands among the funds of its category on one measure, such as "10th of 37" on three-year return. Quartiles split the category into four equal groups, the first holding the top quarter. Ranks compare funds with the same mandate; a rank describes the past, not what comes next.

    Related: SEBI fund categories

    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/know-your-fund/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    Regular plan

    A plan bought through a distributor, such as an agent, broker or bank. It holds the same portfolio as the scheme's Direct plan, but its expense ratio is higher because it includes the commission the fund house pays the distributor. Its NAV is kept separately and is lower than the Direct plan's.

    Related: Direct plan, Expense ratio (TER)

    • AMFI www.mutualfundssahihai.com/en/what-direct-plan-regular-plan checked 02 Oct 2026
    • AMFI www.amfiindia.com/investor/knowledge-center-info?zoneName=DirectPlan checked 02 Oct 2026
    Rolling returns

    Returns over every window of one length in a fund's history, such as every three-year stretch, each starting a little after the last (a week apart on this site). A single figure depends on the dates chosen; rolling returns show the whole range: the best, the worst and how often each kind of result happened.

    Related: Annualised return

    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/rolling-returns/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    SEBI fund categories

    SEBI's 2017 rules sort every open-ended scheme into five groups (equity, debt, hybrid, solution-oriented and other) and into defined categories within them, such as large cap, mid cap or liquid. Each category fixes what a fund must mostly hold, and a fund house may offer one scheme per category, with a few exceptions.

    Related: Large, mid and small cap, Rank in category

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2017/1509103633344.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    Sectors

    The industries the companies in a fund belong to, such as banking, information technology or energy, each shown as a share of the fund's shares. A sectoral fund stays within one industry; a diversified fund spreads across many, so one industry's fortunes move it less.

    Related: Asset mix

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    Sharpe ratio

    Return per unit of risk: the fund's return above a risk-free rate, divided by its volatility. Higher means more return per unit of swing. It sees only the swings that happened, so it can miss rare, sudden risks such as a borrower's default in a debt fund. The risk-free rate is the 91-day Treasury bill yield, averaged over the period.

    Related: Volatility

    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/mutual-fund-risk-metrics/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    SIP (systematic investment plan)

    A fixed amount put into a fund at regular intervals, often each month. Each instalment buys units at that day's NAV, so the same amount buys more units when the NAV is lower and fewer when it is higher, which averages the cost of a unit over time.

    Related: Lump sum, XIRR

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    Units

    What an investor owns in a mutual fund. Money put in buys units at the day's NAV, so ₹10,000 at a NAV of ₹50 buys 200 units. Every unit shares equally in the fund's gains and losses, and units are sold back to the fund at the NAV of the day the request counts for.

    Related: NAV (net asset value), Fund house (AMC)

    • SEBI www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/sep-2024/1727242783639.pdf checked 02 Oct 2026
    • AMFI www.amfiindia.com/investor/knowledge-center-info?zoneName=NetAssetValueNAV checked 02 Oct 2026
    Volatility

    How much a fund's returns swing up and down: the standard deviation of its returns, stated as a yearly percentage. A higher figure means larger swings in both directions. Two funds with the same return can differ widely in how bumpy the way there was. This site measures it from daily prices over each period it shows.

    Related: Sharpe ratio, Deepest fall (maximum drawdown)

    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/mutual-fund-risk-metrics/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    • Zerodha Varsity zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/know-your-fund/ checked 02 Oct 2026
    XIRR

    A yearly return for money that went in on different dates and in different amounts, such as a SIP. Each amount is counted from its own date. For one purchase held more than a year it equals the annualised return; over less than a year it can look extreme, because a short gain is scaled up to a year.

    Related: Annualised return, SIP (systematic investment plan)
